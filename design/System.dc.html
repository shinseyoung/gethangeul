Ink & Hanji

White paper, one neutral ink, one blue. Colour enters three times and no more — the eight option glyphs, the keyword tags, and the season wash on the name card. Structure is hairlines and space, not cards, shadows or rounded corners.

Pigments carry meaning; the blue carries state. Each of the eight option glyphs keeps its own pigment even at rest, so no screen is ever fully grey — and tags use the same pigment at 12% fill with a 30% border. The blue is reserved for the current step, the chosen thing and the stamp.

The name card's season wash

This replaces the ornamental border that keyed off gender. Two reasons: line-art ornament is the hardest thing to keep clean at four sizes, and decorating by gender quietly contradicts the app's own gender-neutral option. The season answer is the most visually expressive of the four, and nobody is offended by winter. One mechanism — a watercolour wash bleeding in from the card edges, a matching mountain at its foot, a matching keyword tag — four moods, no ornament to get wrong.

Rules that keep it honest

Hairlines, not cards 1 px #E4E5E3 separates things. Shadows only under the name card and the primary button — nowhere else.
One accent, used rarely One blue marks the current step, the chosen thing, and the stamp. Colour that means something else — a season, a mood — is a pigment, never the blue. If the accent is everywhere it stops meaning anything.
Ads sit in wells Sunken paper, dashed edge, label outside the frame, never within 24 px of a button. Nothing about an ad may look tappable as content.
44 px minimum Every tap target on mobile. Option tiles run 78 px tall, buttons 48 px, gutters 24 px.
